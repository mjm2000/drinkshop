    Description

    What is it?

    VOLT BAR is a walk-up and counter-service energy drink bar where customers build custom blends from sugar-free bases, natural caffeine sources, and functional add-ins — think boba-bar customization meets esports lounge energy.

    We also stock a diverse bottled energy program: mainstream favorites, zero-sugar lines, and imported cans you won't find at a typical gas station — Japanese, Korean, European, and Latin American brands alongside domestic craft options. Grab one from the cooler on the way out, or stay and build something custom at the bar.

    Unlike generic coffee shops or convenience stores, we own the full energy niche — made-to-order + curated retail under one roof for gamers, gym-goers, and the late-night crowd.

    Where VOLT BAR wins

    No DFW player owns energy-first + in-store lounge + transparent caffeine (mg) + gaming/fitness culture in one concept. Drive-thru chains (7 Brew, Dutch Bros, Swig) win on convenience; Leaf Café wins on wellness; boba shops win on tea. VOLT BAR sits in the gap: a dedicated energy bar with late hours, custom builds, and a scene — not a coffee shop with energy on the side.

    Is food required?

    Energy shop — No. VOLT BAR needs a city food-establishment permit because of custom beverage prep (ice, bases, milk alternatives), but Texas and DFW rules do not require a kitchen or entrees. Bottled/import cans are prepackaged retail. Optional prepackaged snacks (chips, protein bars) add ticket size without a grease trap or hot kitchen.

    Coffee shop — No. Beverage-only coffee is allowed under a standard food-service permit. Pastries and sandwiches are optional for revenue; a full kitchen is a choice, not a license requirement (unless you add alcohol).

    Bar — Yes. Texas TABC generally requires real food service with a mixed beverage permit: commercial cooking equipment, multiple entrees, and food available whenever alcohol is served. Prepackaged snacks alone do not count. Dallas/Frisco zoning also treats 75%+ alcohol revenue as a bar use, not a restaurant.

    Profitability · Cost · Setup

    Energy shop

    Profitability

    • Higher ticket + 65–75% margin
    • Late-night daypart adds revenue hours
    • Unproven category — no benchmark comps
    • Frequency still being validated

    Cost

    • No TABC fees or dram shop insurance
    • Equipment below full espresso/bar build
    • Lounge build-out + ice program add cost
    • ~$242K mid-case per VOLT model

    Setup

    • Same zoning path as coffee (restaurant use)
    • No age verification or liquor overlay
    • Must explain novel use to planners/landlords
    • Late-night may need SUP in some PDs

    Coffee shop

    Profitability

    • Proven daily habit — repeat AM traffic
    • Office corridors = steady lunch volume
    • Lower ticket ($5–8) caps revenue
    • DFW oversaturated — margin pressure

    Cost

    • Can start beverage-only, lower food cost
    • Used espresso gear widely available
    • Espresso machine $8K–25K+
    • Franchise fees if not independent

    Setup

    • Landlords & health dept understand the use
    • Fastest CO / permit path of the three
    • Hard to differentiate in crowded market
    • Weak late-night unless hybrid model

    Alcohol bar

    Profitability

    • Highest $/drink and tip income
    • Strong nightlife corridors (Deep Ellum)
    • 75% alcohol rule — limits food revenue mix
    • Volatile; seasonality & liability risk

    Cost

    • Alcohol inventory turns fast on busy nights
    • Can reuse existing bar build-out shells
    • TABC, higher insurance, security staffing
    • Est. $350–550K+ startup typical

    Setup

    • Nightlife districts expect bar tenants
    • Existing SUP shells available in Deep Ellum
    • SUP/CUP + liquor overlay + TABC timeline
    • Noise/security conditions on permits

    Recommended: Operate as restaurant without drive-in — same bucket as coffee, not a bar. Avoid alcohol unless ready for rezoning + TABC.
