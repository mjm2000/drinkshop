Dallas

City of Dallas · Dallas County · Applies to all Dallas neighborhood candidates

Research summary, not legal advice. Verify zoning, PD overlays, and liquor-control districts for each specific parcel before lease signing. Numbers and fees cited below reflect sources accessed Sep 2026.

Use classification for The Lone Volt

A custom energy drink bar with counter service and optional lounge seating maps most closely to restaurant without drive-in or drive-through service under Dallas Development Code Ch. 51A-4.209 — permitted in commercial, central area, and most industrial districts.[3]

Important: a City food establishment permit (health) does not determine zoning classification.[4] If alcohol, entertainment, or bar-style revenue dominates, the use may be reclassified as bar/lounge/tavern (75%+ alcohol revenue threshold) — likely requiring a Specific Use Permit (SUP).[3][4]

The Lone Volt as beverage-only (no alcohol) avoids TABC and most bar SUP triggers. Standard afternoon and evening retail hours are unlikely to trigger separate late-hours overlay rules; see neighborhoods below if hours ever extend past midnight.[7][9]

Facility requirements (health)

  • Handwash sink, 3-compartment warewashing (if reusable utensils), sanitizer, mop sink[2]
  • Floor drains / hub drains in prep areas (plan review checks this)[2]
  • Commercial refrigeration for concentrates and bottled inventory; ice handling per Texas Food Establishment Rules[2]
  • Grease interceptor — confirm with plan reviewer; beverage-only with minimal food may have reduced requirements but many landlords require one in restaurant shells[2]

Deep Ellum

PD 269 Deep Ellum/Near East Side Planned Development District[5]

  • Restaurant without drive-in service — permitted by right on Tract A (core Deep Ellum) By right[5]
  • Bar, lounge, or tavern — requires SUP SUP[5]
  • Parking: In original buildings, no parking required for first 5,000 sq ft of restaurant floor area; then 1 space per 100 sq ft. On-street parking may count. Outdoor seating under certain covers may be excluded from parking calc (up to 50% of interior area).[6]
  • Late hours (proposed): As of 2024–2026, city and Deep Ellum Foundation discussed requiring a midnight SUP for businesses operating 12 a.m.–6 a.m. — not yet adopted citywide but actively discussed.[9][10] Relevant only if hours extend past midnight; standard retail hours typically avoid this.
  • Noise: Entertainment district; live music venues nearby — expect noise compatibility scrutiny on outdoor speakers and patio use.[9]

Lower Greenville / M Streets

PD 842 Modified Delta Overlay · Late-hours rules apply[8]

  • Restaurants permitted by right in PD 842 commercial areas[8]
  • Late Hour Establishment: Any retail/personal service open between midnight and 6 a.m. requires a Specific Use Permit — must close by midnight without SUP[7][8]
  • Recent SUP examples (e.g., Voodoo Doughnuts) extended hours to 2 a.m. with conditions: max sq ft, customer removal by 2:15 a.m., no outdoor speakers[8]
  • If extending hours past midnight → plan SUP application and hearing timeline (3+ months)

Bishop Arts / Oak Cliff

Verify PD Mix of PD districts and traditional commercial (LC, NC)

  • Restaurant without drive-through generally allowed in commercial districts per Ch. 51A-4.209[3]
  • Bishop Arts has conservation/overlay areas — check for historic facade, sign, and parking restrictions on parcel
  • Lower rent than Uptown but smaller foot traffic; same citywide health/permit process[1][2]
  • Confirm whether property falls under Oak Cliff special purpose or conservation district (affects demolition, signage)

Design District / Trinity Groves

Mixed zoning Industrial/commercial (IR, LC) and PD tracts

  • Restaurant without drive-in permitted in commercial and industrial districts[3]
  • Trinity Groves area uses multiple PDs — parking ratios and use tables vary by PD ordinance; do not assume Deep Ellum rules[6]
  • Destination dining corridor; less natural evening walk-up traffic than Deep Ellum
  • Check DART/I-30 frontage sign ordinances and any design standards in PD documents

Liquor control overlays (if adding alcohol later)

Dallas has D and D-1 Liquor Control Overlay Districts (Ch. 51A-4.503) restricting distance to schools, churches, and other sensitive uses.[11] The Lone Volt MVP is non-alcoholic, but any future energy+cocktail line would require overlay + TABC review.
