Frisco

City of Frisco · Collin & Denton Counties

Research summary, not legal advice. Planned Development (PD) zoning overrides base ordinance on many Frisco parcels — always check GIS first.[3]

Frisco / The Star area

Mix of C-1, C-2, H (Highway), R (Retail), and PD districts near The Star, Legacy West, and Main Street. Event traffic spikes on game days; many pads are drive-through oriented.

  • Restaurant — permitted by right in O-2, R, OTC, H, C-1, C-2, IT, and I districts[4]
  • Drive-through restaurant — permitted where restaurant is permitted, except prohibited in O-2 and OTR (Original Town Retail)[4]
  • VOLT BAR counter + walk-up (no drive-through) fits standard restaurant use[4]
  • Alcohol revenue rule: If ≥75% quarterly gross revenue from on-premise alcohol, use reclassifies to Alcoholic Beverage Establishment (SUP required)[4]
  • PD districts: If parcel is PD, the PD ordinance controls — may impose architectural standards, parking, and use restrictions beyond base code[3]

Health / facility requirements

  • Grease interceptor typically required for food service — confirm with health plan reviewer for beverage-prep-only scope[2]
  • Equipment specification sheets, finish schedules, and menu required with plan submittal[2]
  • Plumbing for ice machine, sinks, and floor drains per adopted codes[2]
  • Separate health permit for each distinct operation at same address[1]

Frisco-specific considerations

  • Stricter suburban standards: Signage, landscaping, and parking often more regulated than urban PD districts[4]
  • Drive-through competition: Zoning favors drive-through pads on highway frontage — in-line retail may have lower rent but less visibility[4]
  • Late-night hours: Suburban noise ordinances and shopping-center lease restrictions may cap hours earlier than urban Dallas targets
  • Event traffic: The Star area — negotiate lease for game-day parking access and trash/security obligations
