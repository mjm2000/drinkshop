Old Town / Vista Ridge / I-35E corridor

Two distinct sub-markets: Historic Old Town (walkable, mixed retail) and Vista Ridge / SH-121 (suburban power centers, drive-through pads).

  • Restaurant — use category in UDC with parking at 1 space per 100 sq ft (stand-alone restaurant)[5]
  • Shopping center exception: If restaurant ≤10% of center sq ft (excluding stand-alone buildings), parking may calculate as retail (1:300) instead of restaurant (1:100)[5]
  • Drive-through: Minimum 4 stacking spaces required when drive-through is part of operation — N/A if The Lone Volt has no drive-through[5]
  • Bar — separate use with 1:100 parking; confirm classification if any alcohol added later[5]
  • Special Use Permits — required for certain uses/districts per UDC Ch. III.9; verify on parcel before lease[5]

Health code highlights (Code of Ordinances Ch. 7)

  • Permit required to operate any food establishment — no operation without valid permit displayed on premises[3]
  • Separate permit and fee for each establishment at same address[3]
  • Exemptions exist for certain low-risk operations (e.g., fountain drinks only, prepackaged food only) — The Lone Volt with custom prep likely does not qualify[3]
  • Dog-friendly patio rules exist if outdoor seating added — separate entrance, signage, self-closing doors[3]

Alcohol distance rules (UDC Ch. VII.4.1)

Lewisville UDC includes distance regulations for sale of alcoholic beverages.[5] The Lone Volt MVP is non-alcoholic; if adding alcohol later, review Section VII.4.1 and TABC requirements for the specific parcel.

Old Town vs. Vista Ridge

Old Town

Historic downtown — better fit for lounge/counter vibe and evening hours. Check historic character standards and on-street parking. Smaller sq ft footprints typical.[5]

Vista Ridge / highway retail

High visibility, drive-by traffic, often end-cap or pad sites. Competes with drive-through beverage model. Easier parking but less "scene" for gaming lounge concept.[5]
