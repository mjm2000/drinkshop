Fort Worth

City of Fort Worth · Tarrant County

Research summary, not legal advice. Confirm base zoning district and any CUP/PD overlays on the exact parcel. Fees per sources accessed Sep 2026.

West 7th / Cultural District

Primary The Lone Volt candidate corridor. Most parcels are zoned mixed-use (MU-2 High Intensity Mixed-Use) or commercial (E/F/G districts). Retail and restaurant uses on ground floor are encouraged in MU-2.[6][5]

  • Restaurant / food service — generally permitted in E, F, G, and MU-2 districts; check Nonresidential Use Table for exact parcel Verify district[4]
  • Urban design overlay: Cultural District/West 7th overlay is under development (2026) — not yet fully adopted. Monitor for future form-based standards on signage, facades, and streetscape[5]
  • Bars/taverns in MU-2: ≤2,000 sq ft by right; larger requires Conditional Use Permit (CUP) with public hearing[8]
  • The Lone Volt (non-alcohol, ~1,200–1,600 sq ft) likely classifies as restaurant/retail food — not bar CUP — but confirm with Planner of Day
  • Parking: Per base district + Chapter 6 development standards; MU-2 has structured parking and mixed-use reductions — verify shared parking in multi-tenant buildings[4][6]
  • Late hours: Entertainment corridor; no citywide midnight SUP like Dallas PD 842, but noise and outdoor amplification may face neighbor complaints — check lease for sound limits

Health code highlights (Ch. 16)

  • No person may operate a food establishment without a current city permit[7]
  • Separate permit for each distinct operation at same address[7]
  • Plans required for new construction, extensive remodel, or change of ownership/conversion[2][7]
  • Classifications include food service establishment (restaurants, snack bars) and bars/lounges[7]
  • Follow Texas Food Establishment Rules for ice, cold holding, handwash, and sanitizer[7]

CO process tips (restaurants)

  • If space was previously a restaurant: new CO for new business ("Occupancy Existing Ordinance") — not a full change-of-use[3]
  • If new use or different from prior: planning review includes parking, signage, landscaping; may trigger commercial building permit[3]
  • Reviews take ~2–3 business days; inspections after approval[3]
  • Register business with TX Secretary of State and obtain sales tax permit first[3]
